Listen for the need beneath the argument.

A reflection on the difference between hearing a position and understanding an experience.

More than the surface topic

A conversation about plans may also be a conversation about feeling considered. A disagreement about chores may include questions about fairness. These are possibilities to be curious about, not hidden meanings you can assume on someone else's behalf.

It can be tempting to explain the other person before asking them. Understanding begins with leaving room for an answer that is different from your own interpretation.

Curiosity is not agreement

You can want to understand a perspective without agreeing with every part of it. You can also want your own experience to be heard without requiring the other person to describe it exactly as you would.

A question to consider after an ordinary disagreement is: what was I hoping they would recognise? Perhaps it was effort, disappointment, uncertainty, or a preference you had not found words for yet.

Keep your boundaries in the picture

Not every difficult interaction is a communication puzzle. Curiosity does not require accepting intimidation, threats, or harm, and this reflection is not intended for unsafe situations. Understanding another person's feelings is not the same as taking responsibility for their behaviour.

If a conversation feels appropriate and safe, you might return to the part you most wanted understood. You do not have to resolve everything in one exchange, and you can seek individual support when you need help considering your circumstances.

A question to take with you

What do I want the other person to understand, beyond the point I am making?

You do not have to answer it today.
