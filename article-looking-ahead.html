When your mind keeps looking ahead.

Making room for the difference between what is here and what is still uncertain.

The conversation that has not happened

An unanswered message can become an imagined conversation. An approaching deadline can become a whole story about what happens afterwards. Before the day has unfolded, you may already feel occupied by its possible endings.

This reflection is not a way to switch worry off. It is simply a chance to describe what has your attention, without demanding that you solve every possibility at once.

Name the uncertainty

Consider a situation that is on your mind. Which parts have actually happened? Which parts are questions? You might notice that 'I have not heard back' and 'I know what they are thinking' are different kinds of statement.

Making that distinction does not guarantee reassurance. Sometimes the honest answer is that you do not know yet. You do not have to turn uncertainty into optimism for the reflection to be worthwhile.

What would be useful to discuss?

Perhaps there is a practical question to ask, a decision that can wait, or a concern you would like to talk through with someone. Consider what would be useful rather than what would make you perfectly certain.

If worry is affecting your daily life, you can bring that experience to a qualified professional. This page is a reflective starting point, not an assessment or a treatment plan. Your circumstances deserve more attention than a short article can offer.

A question to take with you

What do I know about today, and what am I being asked to guess?

You do not have to answer it today.
