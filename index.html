01First contactStart with your words.

Tell us what has been taking up space. You do not need a diagnosis, a complete timeline, or the perfect explanation.

A place to start“Something feels difficult, and I would like to talk.”
02Finding fitMake room for understanding.

We talk through your priorities, preferences, and questions to identify an appropriate starting point. You can ask about the approach, practitioner fit, and practical details.

A useful question“What might this kind of support feel like for me?”
03Shared directionShape a plan together.

Choose a direction around your goals, context, and capacity. Conversation and practical tools can sit alongside each other, with space to say what is and is not useful.

Your voice matters“This is what I would like to feel different.”
04Ongoing careLet the process evolve.

Revisit what is changing, what still needs care, and whether the pace feels right. Progress does not have to follow a straight line.

Keep the conversation open“Can we pause and look at what I need now?”

09 / Before you begin

A little clarity.
A little less unknown.

It is okay to have questions before you feel ready to reach out.

More questions, answered ↗
Do I need to know what to say?

No. A few words about what feels difficult are enough to start a conversation. You can take your time and decide what you feel ready to share.

How do I choose the right support?

Begin with your questions and preferences. Ask about the practitioner's approach, areas of focus, and how sessions work before deciding what feels appropriate.

Can I ask about fees and availability first?

Yes. Use the contact page to ask about current fees, session formats, and appointment availability before making a commitment.

What should I share in an enquiry?

Keep it brief: the kind of support you are exploring and your practical questions. Avoid including sensitive details in an initial contact form.
